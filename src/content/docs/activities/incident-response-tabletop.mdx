A tabletop exercise walks through an incident on paper before one happens in production. The goal is to test your process, not your infrastructure. This activity gives you a scenario and asks you to respond using the framework from lecture.

## The Scenario

**Pinnacle Provisions** — the three-location restaurant chain from the course narrative — has a website outage.

> **14:03:** A customer reports the website is returning a blank page. The support email starts receiving similar reports.
>
> **14:07:** You SSH into the web server. The WordPress container is running, but `docker compose logs wordpress` shows repeated `Error establishing a database connection` messages.
>
> **14:09:** You check the database container: `docker compose ps` shows it is in a restart loop. `docker compose logs db` shows: `[ERROR] InnoDB: Page [page id: space=0, page number=7] log sequence number 12345678 is in the future!`
>
> **14:11:** You realize: a junior engineer ran `docker compose down -v` earlier today to "free up space." All database volumes were deleted.
>
> **14:13:** You locate an S3 backup from this morning at 06:00. Restore time is estimated at 20 minutes.
>
> **14:33:** Restore completes. Service is back. Approximately 8 hours of new orders are lost.

## Questions

1. What severity level (SEV-1 through SEV-4) did you assign? Justify your choice.
2. The incident was caused by a human running the wrong command. The blameless postmortem culture says "don't blame the person." What should the organization change to prevent this class of incident?
3. There were 8 hours of lost orders because the most recent backup was 8 hours old. What would you change about the backup strategy, and what term describes the maximum acceptable data loss?
4. Who would you assign as Incident Commander for this incident if you were one of two engineers on call?

## Deliverable

Submit a PDF to Canvas with:

- Answers to the four questions

Your **full name and student ID** must appear in the PDF header.

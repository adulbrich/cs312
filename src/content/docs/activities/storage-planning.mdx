Every storage decision involves tradeoffs between capacity, performance, availability, and cost. This activity gives you three scenarios and asks you to make and justify real choices.

## Reference: RAID Levels

| RAID Level | Min Drives | Usable Capacity | Fault Tolerance | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| RAID 0 | 2 | 100% of all drives | None | Striping only, no redundancy |
| RAID 1 | 2 | 50% of total raw | 1 drive failure | Full mirror |
| RAID 5 | 3 | (n-1) drives | 1 drive failure | Parity distributed |
| RAID 6 | 4 | (n-2) drives | 2 drive failures | Double parity |
| RAID 10 | 4 | 50% of total raw | 1 drive per mirror pair | Stripe of mirrors |

## Scenario A: Video Surveillance Archive

A security company stores 30 days of HD camera footage. Total data: approximately 48 TB. Requirements:
- Data must survive a single drive failure without data loss
- Cost is the top priority; performance is secondary
- Drives will be in a NAS (Network Attached Storage) device

**Answer:**
1. Which RAID level would you choose? Why?
2. What drive type (HDD or SSD)? Why?
3. How many drives of what size would you specify to achieve at least 48 TB usable? Show your calculation.
4. What is the total raw capacity vs. usable capacity?

## Scenario B: High-Frequency Database

A financial analytics team runs a PostgreSQL database that handles 10,000 transactions per second. Requirements:
- Must survive two simultaneous drive failures
- Read and write latency under 1ms
- The database is 2 TB

**Answer:**
1. Which RAID level would you choose? Why?
2. What drive type and interface? Why?
3. How many drives of what size? Show your calculation.
4. What is the cost tradeoff you are accepting with your choice?

## Scenario C: Developer Workstation

A software engineer wants fast storage for a local VM library and code repositories. Requirements:
- Maximum read/write speed (no redundancy needed — code is in GitHub, VMs are recreatable)
- 4 TB usable capacity
- Quiet and low power

**Answer:**
1. Which RAID level (or no RAID) would you choose? Why?
2. What drive type and form factor?
3. How many drives of what size?
4. What is the single biggest risk of your choice, and how would you mitigate it?

## Cross-Scenario Questions

1. RAID is not a backup. Explain specifically what failure mode RAID does *not* protect against.
2. What is the difference between NAS, SAN, and DAS? Give a one-sentence example use case for each.
3. A RAID 5 array with four 10 TB drives experiences a drive failure. While rebuilding (which takes 12+ hours on large HDDs), what risk exists? What RAID level would reduce this risk?

<Aside type="tip">
For capacity calculations: RAID 5 usable = (number of drives - 1) × drive size. RAID 6 usable = (number of drives - 2) × drive size. RAID 10 usable = total raw ÷ 2.
</Aside>

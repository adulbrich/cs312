Your friend's uncle, who runs a boutique llama-wool sock business ('Sock-it-to-Me Llama Co.'), needs you to set up the network for their new two-room office. He's heard you're a 'computer whiz' and is paying you in socks and artisanal coffee. Using Cisco Packet Tracer, you'll need to design a network that keeps the 'Design' and 'Sales' teams on separate subnets (they argue a lot), connect their server, and get them online. Deliver your Packet Tracer file and a short memo explaining your design so the uncle can understand it. Don't mess it up; llama wool is surprisingly expensive.

Whenever you add devices to your canvas, they will be powered up and act like real devices. This means that for example, when you connect a PC to a switch, it will take some time for the connection to be up and running (green on the connection ends).

Whenever the connection is not yet operational, the connection will be either orange or red.

## Setting Up the Sock-it-to-Me Llama Co. Network

We'll be setting up the network for the boutique llama-wool sock business's new two-room office. As requested, we need to keep the Design and Sales teams on separate subnets since they argue a lot. We'll focus on the essential network elements, though a real business might need additional devices like printers, phones, and enhanced security features.

### Manual VLANs setup

Let's add a few endpoints (5 PCs) on the canvas first: `PC0`, `PC1`, `PC2`, `PC3`, `PC4`.

Let's also add two “access” switches (model 2960): `Switch0` and `Switch1`. You can prefix the switch with `access` to make it clear on the canvas what their purpose is.

Run straight-through cables from the first three PCs to `Switch0` , and the other two endpoints to `Switch1` . Connect endpoints to the FastEthernet ports on the switches, starting from the first port available.

Add another “distribution” switch (also model 2960) called `Switch2` and connect it to our to access switches using cross-over cables. Use one of the GigabitEthernet port on the access switches, and FastEthernet ports on the distribution switch.

<Aside type="note" title="Question 1">
Why are we using different cables to connect our devices together?
</Aside>
Now that the physical connections are ready, we can configure our endpoints. Following the uncle's requirement to keep Design and Sales teams separate, we'll assign them different subnets:

 - `192.168.10.0/24` for DESIGN (the creative folks)
 - `192.168.20.0/24` for SALES (the business folks)

Here are the IP addresses for our Sock-it-to-Me Llama Co. workstations:

|Endpoint |Team |IP Address |
|---|---|---|
|PC0 |Design |192.168.10.100 |
|PC1 |Design |192.168.10.101 |
|PC2 |Design |192.168.10.102 |
|PC3 |Sales |192.168.20.100 |
|PC4 |Sales |192.168.20.101 |

> You might want to rename your PCs to reflect their team assignment, it will help you quickly identify which team each workstation belongs to. You can rename `PC0` to `Design-PC0`, `DESIGN-192.168.10.100` or `VLAN10-192.168.10.100`, for example.

To configure the IP addresses, click on the PC and go to the Desktop tab. Open IP Configuration. Fill the IPv4 field with the correct IP address (see table above). The subnet mask should fill automatically with `255.255.255.0`.

Do that for all endpoints. As you may notice, this is a very time-consuming task. We'll fix that later using a DHCP server.

<Aside type="note" title="Question 2">
Open `PC0` and try pinging `PC1` and then `PC4` through the Command Prompt. What happens? Explain the results you get.
</Aside>
Now that we have our computers on different subnets, let's configure VLANs on the switches.

Click on the switch and go to the CLI tab. Type the following commands:

```text
en # "enable" to enter privileged exec mode 
conf t # "configure terminal" to enter global configuration mode
vlan 10
name DESIGN
vlan 20
name SALES
exit 
exit
show vlan
```

Run these commands on all three switches so that they become aware of the different VLANs.

Now that the VLANs are configured, we can configure each physical port connected to an endpoint. Go to the first access switch, open the config tab, select the interface used to connect to your first endpoint (`FastEthernet0/x`), and switch the port to the correct VLAN.

> If you don't remember which interface your endpoint is connected to, one useful feature to enable is to have the port show up on the logical canvas (go to the app Preferences).

import cptBasicNetworkConfigImage from './network-simulation/cpt-basic-network-config.png';

<FigureWithCaption src={cptBasicNetworkConfigImage} caption="Configure your device's physical ports to the correct VLANs"/>

**Repeat for every PC and the second access switch.** As you might have noticed, we can also do that in the CLI:

```text
interface FastEthernet0/1 # the short-hand is `int f0/1`
switchport access vlan 10
```

The last part is to switch the ports that connects our access switches and our distribution switch to trunk mode. Identify the ports used to connect the switches together and switch that port to trunk mode. The device at the other end of that cable should update it's port mode automatically.

<Aside type="caution" title="Auto-Trunk">
Don't rely on “the other end will auto-trunk.” In Packet Tracer it usually does, but if inter-VLAN traffic is flaky, force `switchport mode trunk` on both sides of inter-switch links.
</Aside>
### VLANs routing

Before enabling routing on our Sock-it-to-Me Llama Co. network, let's prepare for future expansion by configuring two additional VLANs:

 - `192.168.99.0/24` for MANAGEMENT (for network infrastructure devices like servers and network management tools)
 - `192.168.200.0/24` for GUEST_WIFI (for customer wifi access - the uncle mentioned wanting to offer wifi to visiting yarn suppliers)

Add these VLANs to our three existing switches by using the commands shown in the previous steps.

Now it's time to add our routing device. Routing is a layer 3 concern, and switches usually only deal with layer 2 concerns (MAC addresses). Multilayer switches (MLS) add layer 3 and layer 4 capabilities to switches.

Search or select the **3560 MLS** and add it to the canvas. Connect our new MLS to the distribution switch using the proper cable. Change the ports to trunk mode.

Configure all four VLANs on our new MLS switch (using the same commands shown in the previous step).

The next step is to configure the [Switch virtual interface](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Switch_virtual_interface)s (SVI) for each VLAN. The SVI will provide the default gateway for the VLAN and hence allow traffic to be routed.

Open the MLS and go to the CLI tab. Enter the configuration mode then type:

```text
int vlan 10
ip address 192.168.10.1 255.255.255.0
```

Do that for the other three VLANs, making sure you change the default gateway IP address to the right subnet (10, 20, 99, or 200). Once all SVIs are configured, type `ip routing` to enable the feature on the MLS.

You can check that the SVIs and IP routing have been properly configured by typing `show run` in the CLI and scrolling down.

The last thing to do is to configure the default gateway on each endpoint.

Open the PC, go to the Desktop tab, click IP Configuration, and add the default gateway for the VLAN (`192.168.x.1` replace `x` with the correct subnet). **Do that for all endpoints.**

### Static IP Routing and Internet Connectivity

The uncle wants his sock business to have internet access for online orders and social media marketing. Let's configure routing between our MLS and an edge router that will connect to the "internet" (simulated by Cisco Packet Tracer's cloud).

Let's start by adding the edge router to our canvas. Search or select the 4331 router and rename it to `Edge-Router`. Connect your new router to the MLS using the right cable.

Open the config tab on the router and power on the port that is connected to the MLS (by default ports are off). Next, fill the IP Configuration pane:

 - IPv4 Address: `172.16.1.1`
 - Subnet mask: `255.255.255.252`

<Aside type="note" title="Question 4">
What is the CIDR notation of that IP address and subnet mask? Explain why we chose that subnet in this situation.
</Aside>
Next open your MLS CLI and de-activate switching on the port connected to the router. Enter the configuration mode then type:

```text
int g0/2 # replace with the correct port
no switchport
```

Next go in the Config tab and change the IP Configuration for that port to:

 - IPv4 Address: `172.16.1.2`
 - Subnet mask: `255.255.255.252`

On the 3560 MLS, add a default route so all unknown traffic (i.e., the internet) goes to the edge router:

```text
ip route 0.0.0.0 0.0.0.0 172.16.1.1
```

Your SVIs (VLAN 10/20/99/200) are already the default gateways for clients; this route simply teaches the MLS where to push non-local traffic.

Now, let's simulate the internet through an Internet Service Provider (ISP) router. Add another 4331 router to the canvas and rename it to `ISP-Router`. Connect it to the `Edge-Router`. 

We'll also add a server that will act as a DNS server. Add a server to the canvas, rename it to `Public-DNS-Server`, and connect it to the `ISP-Router`.

> This is a very crude approximation of how the internet works, but it will suffice for our purposes.

Open the `ISP-Router` and configure it:

```text
conf t
int g0/0/0 # change with the correct port
  ip address 203.0.113.1 255.255.255.252
  no shutdown
int g0/0/1 # change with the correct port
  ip address 198.51.100.1 255.255.255.0
  no shutdown
end
wr
```

Open the `Public-DNS-Server` and go to the Desktop tab. Fill the following in the IP Configuration:

- IPv4 Address: `198.51.100.53`
- Subnet Mask: `255.255.255.0`
- Default Gateway: `198.51.100.1`
- DNS Server: `198.51.100.53` (self)

Next, go to the Services tab, select DNS, and switch it “On”. Add a new entry with the following:

- Name: `llama.example`
- Type: `A` record (default)
- Address: `198.51.100.53`

Make sure that HTTP is also “On” in the Services tab.

Finally, we'll configure our `Edge-Router` WAN, LAN, and NAT.  

```text
en
conf t
int g0/0/1 # change with the correct port
  ip address 203.0.113.2 255.255.255.252
  no shutdown
  ip nat outside
int g0/0/0 # change with the correct port
  ip nat inside
exit

ip access-list standard LAN-NETS
  permit 192.168.10.0 0.0.0.255
  permit 192.168.20.0 0.0.0.255
  permit 192.168.99.0 0.0.0.255
  permit 192.168.200.0 0.0.0.255
exit

ip nat inside source list LAN-NETS interface g0/0/1 overload
ip route 0.0.0.0 0.0.0.0 203.0.113.1
ip route 192.168.0.0 255.255.0.0 172.16.1.2
end
wr
```

<Aside type="note" title="Question 5">
What does the `ip route` command do? Why are we using `0.0.0.0/0` as the default route?
</Aside>

### DHCP Configuration

As we've seen, adding endpoints on our network is painful, as we need to keep track of individual IP addresses on each subnet. To solve that problem, let's configure a DHCP server and enable the DHCP client on our endpoints.

Let's add a server to our Canvas, and connect it to the MLS.

This is a “management” device: update the port on the MLS to VLAN 99.

Open the server and configure the following:

 - IPv4 Address: `192.168.99.100`
 - Subnet Mask: `255.255.255.0`
 - Default Gateway: `192.168.99.1`
 - DNS Server: `8.8.8.8` (Google's public DNS for internet connectivity)

In the Services tab, select DHCP and turn it “On”.

Now you need to add pools for our two departments' VLANs. Let's start with VLAN 10:

 - Pool Name: VLAN 10 Pool
 - Default Gateway: `192.168.10.1`
 - DNS Server: `8.8.8.8`
 - Start IP Address: `192.168.10.200`
 - Subnet Mask: `255.255.255.0`
 - Maximum Number of Users: 50

Finally click “Add”. **Repeat for VLAN 20 by changing the subnet.**

Access your MLS' CLI and add the DHCP helper for both VLANs. For the Design team (VLAN 10), enter the configuration mode and type:

```text
int vlan 10
ip helper-address 192.168.99.100
```

**Repeat for the Sales team (VLAN 20).**

Let's test our DHCP setup by adding two new workstations, one for each team:

 - Add a PC to the Canvas and rename it to `Design-New-PC`
 - Connect it to the Design access switch
 - Open the switch and change the VLAN of the port connected to the new PC to VLAN 10
 - Open the PC. In the Config tab, choose DHCP in the Gateway/DNS IPv4 pane.

**Repeat for a Sales team PC, connecting it to the Sales access switch and setting the port to VLAN 20.**

Fast-forward and check that the new workstations have received appropriate IP addresses from their respective DHCP pools.

### Optional: Wireless Setup for Guest Access

<Aside type="caution" title="Performance Warning">
This section is optional due to Cisco Packet Tracer's notoriously slow performance with wireless configurations. If you choose to implement this, be prepared for significant delays and potential crashes.
</Aside>
The uncle mentioned that yarn suppliers occasionally visit the office and might appreciate guest WiFi access. If you want to challenge yourself (and have patience), you can add wireless capability to the Sock-it-to-Me Llama Co. network.

Start by adding a Wireless LAN Controller (WLC) (3504) to our canvas, and connect it to your our MLS. Set the port to Access for VLAN 99.

Open the WLC, go to the Config tab, then the Management interface. Type in the following in the IP Configuration pane:

 - IPv4 Address: `192.168.99.101`
 - Subnet Mask: `255.255.255.0`
 - Default Gateway: `192.168.99.1`
 - DNS Server: `8.8.8.8`

Fast-forward then open a wired endpoint (PC), go to the Desktop tab, and open a web browser. Go to [http://192.168.99.101](http://192.168.99.101). **Be patient, this is very slow.**

Once the page has loaded, it's time to create an account. Choose an admin username and password and click Start.

In the Set Up Your Controller section, choose a name for the system, make sure obvious settings are correct, and type the following:

 - Management IP Address: `192.168.99.101`
 - Subnet Mask: `255.255.255.0`
 - Default Gateway: `192.168.99.1`

Click next.

In the Create Your Wireless Networks section, choose a network name (it will be your SSID), and choose a passphrase (WPA2). Click Next, Next, and finally Apply. The WLC will now reboot.

In the meantime, go to your DHCP server and add a pool for your VLAN 200 (Wifi).

 - Pool Name: VLAN 200 Pool
 - Default Gateway: `192.168.200.1`
 - DNS Server: `8.8.8.8`
 - Start IP Address: `192.168.200.100`
 - Subnet Mask: `255.255.255.0`
 - Maximum Number of Users: 100
 - **WLC Address: `192.168.99.101`**

Connect to your MLS and add a helper for your VLAN:

```text
int vlan 200
ip helper-address 192.168.99.100
```

It's time to add our Wireless Access Points (look for LAP-PT, or Light Weight Access Point). Add two to your canvas, and connect them to whichever switches. Make sure to change the port on the switch to VLAN 200. (You get the gist.) Click on your LAPs and plug them in (drag and drop the power cord in the Physical tab, see screenshot below).

Make sure your LAPs are set to DHCP in the Config tab; fast-forward until they have received an IP address (i.e., the default gateway and DNS server have been populated).

Now that we have our LAPs up and running, let's log back in to our WLC. Go to any PC and open the web browser, this time type [http**s**://192.168.99.101](https://192.168.99.101) (note the S). Log in.

Fast-forward a bit, and your device should connect to one of the two LAPs.
